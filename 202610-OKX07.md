<h2>1. What Is a Cross-Chain Transfer? Why Is It Prone to Errors?</h2>
<p>A cross-chain transfer means: transferring the same asset through different blockchain networks.</p>

<p>For example: USDT can exist on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ethereum (ERC20)</li>
<li>BNB Chain (BEP20)</li>
<li>Tron (TRC20)</li>
<li>Arbitrum</li>
<li>Polygon</li>
<li>Solana</li>
</ul>

<p>Although they are all named USDT, different networks actually belong to different blockchains.</p>

<p>This is also where beginners most easily make mistakes: "the coin is right, but the network is wrong."</p>

<p>For example: you want to transfer USDT, choose ERC20 when withdrawing, but the receiving address only supports TRC20. This situation may cause the asset to fail to arrive normally.</p>

<hr>

<h2>2. Most Common Reasons for OKX Cross-Chain Transfer Failure</h2>

<p><strong>1. Wrong network</strong><br>
This is the most common problem. For example: when transferring USDT, the sender chooses Ethereum ERC20, while the receiver only supports TRC20. Although both are USDT, the two chains are completely different. Before trading, be sure to confirm: coin, network and receiving address must match.</p>

<p><strong>2. Insufficient Gas Fee</strong><br>
On-chain transactions require Gas. Different networks require different Gas Tokens:</p>

<thead>
<tr>
<th>Network</th>
<th>Gas Token</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Ethereum</td>
<td>Requires ETH</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>BNB Chain</td>
<td>Requires BNB</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Solana</td>
<td>Requires SOL</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Tron</td>
<td>Requires TRX</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Polygon</td>
<td>Requires MATIC</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p>If the wallet doesn't have enough Gas, even with USDT, the transfer may not complete.</p>

<p><strong>3. Wrong address format</strong><br>
Different public chains have different address formats. For example: Ethereum addresses usually start with 0x, Solana address format is different, and Tron addresses usually start with T. When copying an address, be sure to confirm it is complete.</p>

<p><strong>4. Blockchain network congestion</strong><br>
Sometimes the transaction doesn't fail, it's just slower to confirm. Especially during periods of sharp market volatility, popular airdrops and increased on-chain activity, transaction confirmation times may be extended.</p>

<h2>5. How to Avoid OKX Cross-Chain Transfer Errors?</h2>

<p><strong>Test with a small amount on the first transfer</strong><br>
Don't transfer all your assets the first time. It's recommended to transfer a small amount first, and after confirming success, make larger transfers.</p>

<p><strong>Don't just look at the coin name</strong><br>
Many beginners see USDT and assume it will definitely work. In reality: USDT + network is a complete asset.</p>

<p><strong>Keep Gas Tokens</strong><br>
When using a Web3 Wallet, don't swap all your ETH, BNB, SOL. Keep a small amount of Gas to avoid being unable to operate later.</p>

<p><strong>Save transaction records</strong><br>
When a problem occurs, TxID is the most important information. Don't delete transaction records or screenshots.</p>
